# Miku News Story Structures

Use these patterns as functional scaffolds, not rigid paragraph counts. Every paragraph must advance either news value, credibility, reader usefulness, or the next step.

## Editorial logic

Build a release around this sequence:

> Timely public or reader context → a focused problem → expert, organization, or action → usable evidence and method → relevant offer or next step → wider value.

The goal is not to disguise promotion. Give the reader a useful perspective before asking for attention, registration, or contact.

## Headline patterns

- **Pain point or tension + expert perspective**: Make the reader situation specific, then name the relevant authority.
- **Trend or data point + practical implication**: Use only a sourced number; otherwise describe the trend without false precision.
- **Person's turning point + new contribution**: Best for books, new programs, or feature stories.
- **Event outcome + the one industry insight it established**: Best for recap releases.
- **Seasonal situation + usable method**: Best for lifestyle or service content.

Prefer a clear outcome or question over vague adjectives. Keep the central claim supportable by the body.

## 1. Issue-led expert commentary

1. Headline and image caption.
2. Timely trend, policy change, market shift, or familiar reader tension.
3. Why the tension matters to the chosen audience.
4. Expert's diagnosis and relevant credentials.
5. Method, plain-language explanation, case, or practical examples.
6. Program, service, book, or initiative that extends the solution.
7. One memorable closing perspective or CTA.

Use this for AI adoption, labor, regulation, entrepreneurship, housing, finance, aging, and other knowledge-led topics.

## 2. Person, book, course, or brand story

1. Reader problem or industry gap.
2. Person's meaningful experience or turning point.
3. Credentials that establish a right to speak on the topic.
4. Concrete client, learner, or field case.
5. New book, course, program, or collaboration.
6. Point of view and next step.

Do not begin with a long résumé. Make the personal story serve the current news reason.

## 3. Event preheat

1. Current market or social context.
2. Date, venue, organizer, theme, and key audience.
3. One speaker's distinctive insight or preview case.
4. What attendees can learn, compare, or take away.
5. Registration CTA and verified link.

Give one useful preview, not only a speaker list.

## 4. Event recap

1. Event outcome with exact attendance terminology and date.
2. Organizer, partners, theme, and why it mattered.
3. One or two exclusive insights, examples, or quotes from the event.
4. A distinct angle for each follow-up speaker story.
5. Future relevance or next action.

Treat the main recap as a broad industry story. Make each follow-up story stand alone rather than repeating the same attendance number and organizer description.

## 5. Public-interest, charity, or ESG action

1. Date, place, beneficiary need, and concrete action.
2. Beneficiary's mission, scale, or first-hand quote.
3. Donated items, amount, service, or renovation outcome, including why it meets the need.
4. Donor's local or personal connection and attributed perspective.
5. Measurable impact and a suitable way for readers to participate, if any.

Use formal, respectful terminology preferred by the beneficiary organization. Obtain consent for private stories, photos, and quotes.

## 6. Seasonal practical guide

1. Immediate season or life-stage situation.
2. Common reader difficulty and a corrective principle.
3. Three to five genuinely usable steps.
4. A realistic habit or safety reminder.
5. Expert context or optional resource.

Let the practical advice carry the story. Keep commercial information light.

## Evidence and attribution rules

- Attribute facts to a named source; include source date, measurement period, and scope where possible.
- Distinguish an external fact from an expert observation and an organization's own result.
- Explain what a percentage, saving, growth figure, ranking, or customer outcome measures. Avoid implying a universal result from one case.
- Use neutral descriptions unless an independent source supports a comparative claim.
- For legal, investment, medical, or policy topics, avoid individualized advice and ask for professional review when necessary.

## Release-ready closing block

Add only when requested or supplied:

```text
了解更多：[官方連結]
媒體聯絡人：[姓名／單位／電話／Email]
圖片／受訪資料：[連結]
```

Use one primary CTA. For a media release, contact and source material are usually more useful than several sales links.
