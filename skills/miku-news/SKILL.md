# Miku News

Create a release that earns attention through a timely reader problem, then makes the expert, organization, event, or action the credible answer. Keep public value ahead of promotion.

## Start from the brief

Extract or infer only these essentials:

1. News hook: current event, season, policy, trend, or reader situation.
2. Primary reader and desired outlet: consumer, industry, business, local, lifestyle, or owned channel.
3. New fact: announcement, action, event, result, point of view, or case.
4. Spokesperson: role, relevant experience, signature perspective, and attributed quote.
5. Evidence: source, date, scope, method, customer case permission, and usable images.
6. Intended next step: learn more, register, interview, donate, or none.

If a consequential fact is missing, do not invent it. Draft with concise `[待確認]` placeholders and list the facts needed for release. Treat legal, financial, medical, investment, policy, and performance claims as high-verification content.
Copy supplied names, titles, venues, and URLs exactly; do not normalize or guess at a proper noun. Verify dates, amounts, percentages, scopes, and derived comparisons exactly in research notes, then decide whether the precise value itself carries the news under the numeric rules below.

## Research the principal person

When a person anchors the release, research that person before drafting:

1. Search the web for the person's name together with the current topic, organization, project, and known role. Review past news coverage and published press releases, not only the person's own biography.
2. Use at least two relevant news reports when available, plus a primary profile or official source when useful. Confirm that similarly named people are not being conflated.
3. Extract only experience that explains why this person can credibly address the current issue: a turning point, field experience, prior initiative, observed case pattern, or sustained professional focus.
4. Add one concise paragraph to the body that connects this experience to the present news angle. Do not insert a detached résumé or list of titles.
5. If reliable prior coverage is unavailable, use the strongest verifiable primary source and do not invent a history.

## Select the story form

Read [structures.md](references/structures.md) before drafting. Choose one primary form and do not combine several promotional objectives in one release:

- Issue-led expert commentary
- Person, book, course, or brand-story launch
- Event preheat or post-event recap
- Public-interest, charity, or ESG action
- Seasonal practical guide

## Drafting workflow

1. State the news angle in one sentence before writing.
2. Always provide exactly 3 distinct headline options for every new, revised, or continued press-release draft. Include all 3 options even when the user supplies a working headline or asks to revise only the body. Make the news hook and expert angle immediately clear in each option. When the evidence supports it, make the three options editorially different rather than synonymous: one result-led, one mechanism or reframing-led, and one human-impact or knowledge-transfer-led.
3. Write a concise image caption after the title when an image is available: subject, what it shows, source attribution.
4. Start the body directly with reader-relevant context. Do not prefix it with `【台北訊】` or any other location-based dateline unless the user explicitly requests one.
5. Introduce the spokesperson or organization as soon as the news action requires it.
6. Give the expert a distinct perspective, then substantiate it with a specific mechanism, case, process, comparison, or attributed data.
7. Include the required experience paragraph, drawing on the prior-coverage research and tying it directly to the expert's authority on this issue.
8. Place course, product, organization, or service details after the reader has received useful information. Use one clear, low-friction CTA only when it serves the release goal.
9. End with a forward-looking takeaway, public value, or next step. Include media contact, source links, and photo links only when supplied or requested.

## AI and operational-transformation stories

When the story concerns AI adoption, workflow automation, digital transformation, or enterprise know-how, use this progression when it fits the evidence:

1. Open with the market tension, then make it visible through two or three familiar workflow fragments such as orders in spreadsheets, customer questions in messaging tools, or inventory in a legacy system. State the business consequence without vague technology hype.
2. Introduce the person and team through the field action they took: whom they entered, what they listened to, and what they connected. Use age or generational identity only when verified and when it creates a meaningful human contrast.
3. Put the measurable result early when it is the new fact. Attribute it to the organization or spokesperson and state the measurement period, scope, and method near first use.
4. Write the experience paragraph as a causal path: the earlier problem the person tried to solve, the capability developed, and how it led to the present method. Avoid generic lines such as “success was no accident” unless the following evidence proves the connection.
5. Let the quote express an operating principle, then show it working across concrete industries or workflow examples. The reader should understand why a chatbot or isolated tool is insufficient.
6. Introduce the platform only after the problem, result, person, and method are clear. Explain what knowledge it converts, which small process a company can start with, and how later processes are added.
7. Close with a conceptual shift that returns to the reader's stakes—for example, from chasing tools to preserving organizational knowledge, or from an AI demonstration to stable daily capacity.

Use vivid headline metaphors such as “automatic employee” only when the body promptly defines the actual tasks, rules, permissions, and human exceptions. Do not imply independent judgment or employment replacement beyond the evidence.

## Publish precise numbers deliberately

Default rule: **verify precisely, publish approximately**. Use natural magnitudes when the exact value adds false certainty, distracts from the public value, or lacks a release-ready source.

- Keep exact dates, amounts, percentages, periods, and scopes in research notes so the factual basis remains auditable.
- In headlines, captions, and body copy, replace exact calendar dates with natural time language: `2027年2月17日` becomes `明年二月`; a recent exact date becomes `本月中旬` or `日前`; several session dates become `近期多場`.
- Replace exact money with a truthful magnitude: `新台幣1,280元` becomes `千元左右`; `1億元` becomes `上億元`. Do not round across a meaningful threshold.
- Replace percentages with a sourced range or natural proportion: `65%` becomes `超過六成`; `48%` becomes `近半數`. Preserve the direction and scale of the source.
- Do not let generalization change legal effect, chronology, eligibility, or the meaning of a result. A source or registration URL may remain exact, but its visible label should not repeat the precise number.

Keep an exact date, amount, percentage, count, or period in the headline or body when the user explicitly wants a data-led release and the number is itself the news. All of the following must be true:

- identify who supplied or calculated the figure;
- state its measurement period, population or customer scope, definition, and method close to first use;
- label estimates, self-reported results, projections, and unaudited figures as such;
- distinguish labor-hours converted into cost from actual cash savings, layoffs, revenue, or audited financial results;
- verify any derived average, per-customer result, full-time-equivalent comparison, or other denominator-based claim mathematically before publication;
- add a compact caveat without allowing the caveat to overwhelm the story.

If the exact customer count or comparison benchmark is unavailable, do not derive a precise per-customer or “equivalent to employees” claim from an approximate count. Generalize that comparison or mark it `[待確認]`. A precise figure may make a strong headline, but attribution and limitations must remain visible in the body.

## Approval and Word export

When the user's next substantive reply after a Miku News draft or revision consists only of approval such as `ok`, `OK`, or `ＯＫ` (ignoring surrounding whitespace and punctuation), treat it as both approval of the latest complete version and an instruction to export that version as a Word `.docx` file. Do not ask for confirmation again. Do not trigger this workflow when `ok` refers to something other than a Miku News draft.

- Use a headline the user previously selected. If the user has not selected one, use headline option 1.
- Export only the publication-ready article: the selected headline, image caption when available, body, and any supplied release-ready contact or source block. Omit unused headline options, the news-angle sentence, research notes, verification commentary, caveats, and editing notes.
- If the latest draft still contains `[待確認]` placeholders or consequential unverified claims, explain what must be resolved instead of exporting it as final.
- Use the document-creation skill when available, follow its render-and-verify workflow, and correct visible layout problems before delivery.
- Save the finished file in the current task's user-facing output location with a concise filename derived from the principal person or organization and topic. Return a clickable link to the `.docx` file.

## House voice

- Write in clear Traditional Chinese for Taiwan: warm, confident, educational, and concrete.
- Make the opening immediately readable and emotionally relevant: start with a familiar situation, an unexpected contrast, a consequential question, or a human stake that creates forward pull.
- Use concrete verbs, lived consequences, and varied sentence rhythm. Let each short paragraph create a reason to continue, while avoiding melodrama, fearmongering, and empty clickbait.
- Prefer a progression from concrete friction to measurable consequence to method. A compact list of contrasting industries or workflow scenes can establish that a problem is systemic, but each example must advance the same news angle.
- Favor short paragraphs of one function each. Keep the body at or below 1,000 Chinese characters; target roughly 750–950. Headline options, image caption, source links, and the closing contact block do not count toward this limit.
- Use a memorable expert line or named framework only when it explains the story. Define specialist terms in plain language on first use.
- Make the expert's experience relevant to the claim; avoid biography dumps.
- Use strong claims sparingly. Replace generic praise with observable facts, process detail, generalized time language, and attributed results.
- Keep quotation marks for authentic, attributable statements. Use one diagnostic quote and, where useful, one practical quote.
- Preserve privacy: anonymize personal cases unless explicit permission is supplied.

## Quality gate

Before finalizing, verify that the release:

- includes exactly 3 distinct headline options;
- has one primary news reason and one intended reader;
- documents web research into the principal person's prior coverage and includes one relevant experience paragraph;
- separates external facts, spokesperson opinion, and organizational claims;
- keeps every numeric claim traceable to an exact source value, period, scope, and definition in research notes;
- generalizes exact dates, money, percentages, and counts by default; when a precise figure is deliberately retained as the news hook, attributes it and supplies its period, scope, definition, method, and limitations;
- recalculates derived averages and comparisons from the verified denominator rather than from phrases such as `五十多家`, and defines the benchmark behind employee-equivalent claims;
- keeps the body at or below 1,000 Chinese characters;
- opens with tension or human stakes and sustains emotional, non-sensational forward momentum;
- avoids unsupported superlatives, guaranteed outcomes, and misleading comparisons;
- uses the correct event-count concept (for example, registration, check-in, or attendance) while editorially generalizing the figure;
- gives public-interest stories the beneficiary's need and impact before the donor's promotion;
- includes no internal comments, editing notes, placeholders, or unconfirmed links;
- is ready to paste into an email or media system.

When revising a user draft, retain verified facts and the intended story angle. Explain only material structural changes unless the user requests line editing.
